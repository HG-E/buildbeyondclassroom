    What every learner does

    Four things, in this order of importance.

    A

    Builds with AI

    Learners use AI tools to design, build and publish something real.

    B

    Finds and checks a community problem

    The need is confirmed with the people who live with it. It is never assumed.

    C

    Makes the business case in their own field

    Who benefits, who pays, what it costs, what rules apply and who else already serves the need.

    D

    Owns the result

    The work belongs to the learner and goes with them, in open formats.

    What makes it different

    A shipped project is the measure.

    Success is a solution in use

    Real people use it, and a community partner confirms it. Attendance does not count.

    A person guides every group

    At a hub, in a hybrid format or in live online sessions. There is no self-paced online course at the core.

    AI helps you learn. It does not think for you.

    Short checks with the tool closed show whether you learned or only produced.

    Light and open

    Programme content is open and works on weak connections. Information about learners is protected.

    The programme

    Three journeys, three levels, one ladder.

    People learn to use AI best by building something real that helps their community.

    Three journeys

    Journey

    Explore

    A first look at how a field works as a business and which path might suit you. Facilitator-led, and offered only after local advisers have cleared age and consent rules.

    Journey, the core

    Build

    A team works through a real community problem from need to launch, with a facilitator. The first pilot is an eight-week facilitated Build journey for adults aged 18 and over.

    Journey

    Launch

    Taking a finished solution to real users and towards funding or income.

    Three levels

    The levels follow UNESCO's AI competency framework for students and the OECD and European Commission AI literacy framework, so schools and funders can see where BBC fits.

    Level 1

    Understand and use

    Use AI tools well and safely.

    Level 2

    Apply and build

    Use them to build solutions.

    Level 3

    Create and scale

    Take a solution further, with a verified portfolio and a path to funding or work.

    A ladder of skills

    Tools are supporting actors. Each rung describes what a learner can do.

      Who can take part

      • BBC is for learners of different ages and backgrounds. Learners under 13 take part only in facilitator-led sessions.
      • The first pilot is for adults aged 18 and over. Work with younger learners begins only after local legal advice has cleared the rules.
      • The plan is to keep access free or low cost for learners, with institutions and funders paying for cohorts. This is still being tested and no prices have been set.

      The facilitator route

      Facilitators are trained and certified. The route has four stages.

      1. Participate
        Join a cohort and see it from the inside.
      2. Co-run
        Share a cohort with a certified facilitator.
      3. Lead
        Run a cohort with support.
      4. Certify
        Become certified to run cohorts and train others.

      The Community Problem Studio

      From a real need to a solution in use.

      A facilitator plans each cycle. Teams work through eight steps. Pick a step to see who is involved and how AI may be used.

      Tutoralways allowed, at every step Co-builderallowed in steps 4 to 6 Own workAI may not be the author in steps 2, 7 and 8

      Three checks with the tool closed

      At the start, middle and end of a cycle, learners complete a short check without AI. They show whether someone has learned or only produced.

      Staying safe

      • A Do-No-Harm Check is signed off by the facilitator before a solution reaches users.
      • Teams stop and ask the facilitator if AI would be the only source of medical, legal or financial advice.
      • Teams stop if a design needs personal data it does not need, or would profile people.

      Hubs and partners

      Use the most human contact a site allows.

      BBC is delivered through facilitated cohorts. Virtual sessions are added only to reach what a hub cannot.

      Ways to run a cohort

      A hub can be a university room, a school classroom, a library, an NGO centre or a community hall. Hubs aim to provide shared devices so no learner needs to own one.

      What a hub needs before it opens

      Required

      A certified facilitator

      Young people take part only in sessions run by a certified facilitator.

      Required

      A signed data protocol

      With a named data owner, before any learner information is kept.

      What every partner agrees to

      Safeguarding

      Of young people, in every session and every hub.

      Data

      Data protection, with a named data owner.

      Open content and the BBC name

      Programme content is open, and the BBC name is used fairly.

      Learner ownership

      Learners own their own work.

      AI use

      AI is used within the programme's rules: tutor always, co-builder in steps 4 to 6, never the author where the work must be the team's own.

      Standards and reporting

      Including a certified facilitator at every cohort.

      Independence and exit

      Payment buys no say over content, learner data or evaluation findings.

      Safeguarding and data

      Content is open. Information about people is protected.

      The rules, in plain words.

      Safeguarding

      • Learners under 13 take part only in facilitator-led sessions.
      • Young people take part only in sessions run by a certified facilitator.
      • The first pilot is for adults aged 18 and over.
      • The facilitator signs a Do-No-Harm Check before a solution reaches users.
      • Every hub has a signed data protocol and a named data owner before it opens.

      What this website does

      • No accounts, no forms that save information and no analytics.
      • No tracking cookies and no third-party scripts.
      • It can keep a copy of its public pages on your device so it works offline. You can clear it in your browser settings.
      • If you email BBC, we use your message only to reply.

      Eight rules for learner information

      How results will be measured

      • Impact is counted at community results: where the partner confirms the solution is in use, at step 8 and again at day 90.
      • At the start, records are kept on paper and in one spreadsheet per hub, using codes rather than names where possible.
      • An independent evaluator agrees the design and thresholds before week 1.
      • BBC will not claim outcomes it has not measured.
      BBC has not yet confirmed which data protection laws apply in each pilot country. Local legal advice is being arranged before any learner records are kept beyond paper and a hub spreadsheet.

      Principles
      
      
      
        
      Show the mechanism
      The Studio track is the product. It appears on the home page, then in full on its own page, with the same colours and words.
      
        
      Colour means a rule
      Three role colours say how AI may be used. They are never used for decoration, and each also carries a text label.
      
        
      Say only what is decided
      Open decisions show as open. No prices, no outcomes and no launch dates are invented.
