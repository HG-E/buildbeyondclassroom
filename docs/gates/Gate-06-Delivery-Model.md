# Build Beyond Classroom: Delivery Model (Gate 6)

Oct 14, 2026 · Ekene

## The delivery choice

Build Beyond Classroom is delivered through facilitated cohorts in three modes: physical hubs, hybrid, and virtual with live facilitation. A fourth option, self-paced online learning, is not a delivery mode for the core programme. The Gate 1 brief found that online-only courses lose most learners, and MIT researchers report that low MOOC completion has not improved in six years. The programme's measure is a shipped project, and projects ship when people work in teams with a facilitator and a community partner.

One rule chooses the mode for any site: **use the most human contact the site's resources allow, and add virtual only to reach what a hub cannot.** In practice:

1. Start with a physical hub where a safe space, a facilitator and a community partner exist within reach of learners.
2. Add a virtual layer to link hubs, share the problem library and bring in mentors and field experts from elsewhere.
3. Run a fully virtual cohort only when learners cannot reach any hub, and then only with live facilitation, small teams and a local community partner for each team.

The same curriculum, templates, checks and safeguards apply in every mode. What changes is where people meet and what devices and connectivity are available.

## The three modes compared

| | Physical hub | Hybrid | Virtual, live-facilitated |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| How it runs | The cohort meets in person each week at a hub; teams work together with a facilitator present; partners visit in person | In-person weekly session at a hub, plus virtual sessions for experts, cross-hub reviews and remote teammates | Live online sessions in small teams; each team has a local community partner |
| Best for | Low-connectivity sites, first-time learners, the Explore add-on | Mid and high-connectivity sites; students who also work or commute far | Learners far from any hub; high-connectivity sites |
| Needs | A safe space with power, a facilitator, shared devices or paper templates, and partner access | A hub, plus a reliable connection and a room set up for virtual sessions | Own device, stable connection, a live facilitator and a local partner for every team |
| Connectivity tier | Offline or low bandwidth | Low bandwidth to full | Full only |
| Main risk | Reach is limited to people who can travel to the hub | Unequal experience between people in the room and those online; extra load on the facilitator | The lowest completion, device and data costs falling on the learner, and harder safeguarding for minors |
| Safeguards | Facilitator present throughout | Facilitator present at the hub; virtual sessions recorded only with consent | Under-13s only in facilitator-led sessions with an adult present; no private one-to-one online contact |

The Gate 2 age bands and the Gate 3 do-no-harm rules apply in all three modes. In virtual delivery, no facilitator or mentor contacts a minor privately, and every session has more than one adult or is recorded with consent.

A small core team sets standards and trains facilitators; regional partners host and supervise hubs; hubs run cohorts at the connectivity tier that suits them; and a virtual layer links the hubs. Standards, training and the toolkit flow down at every level, and results and corrections flow back up.

## What a hub needs

A hub is any place where a facilitated cohort meets regularly: a university room, a school classroom, a library, an NGO centre or a community hall. The programme certifies a hub against a short checklist, so a new country can open one without redesigning anything.

| Requirement | Offline tier | Low-bandwidth tier | Full-connectivity tier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Space and safety | A safe, accessible room with a named responsible adult | Same | Same |
| Power | Reliable enough for the session, or a charging plan | Same, plus a backup for devices | Same |
| Devices | Shared devices for teams, with paper first for early steps | Shared devices; learners' own phones where terms allow | Learners' own devices where allowed, with a few shared ones |
| Connectivity | None needed for the core method; occasional sync | A connection used in short, planned bursts | A stable connection for tools and virtual sessions |
| Tools | Small offline models and offline editors | Lighter tools, with downloads done in advance | The full category list from the Gate 3 toolkit |
| Templates | Printed Gate 3 templates and Field Cards | Printed and digital | Digital, printable on demand |
| Community partner access | Partner visits in person | In person, with messaging between visits | In person or by video |
| Records | Paper logs, entered later | Paper and simple digital forms | Digital logs, with the data protocol applied |

Two requirements are the same in every tier and are checked before a hub opens: a trained, certified facilitator, and a signed data protocol with a named data owner. A hub that fails either does not run a cohort.

## Facilitators and capacity

Facilitators are the programme's scarcest resource, so the delivery model is built around them. Gate 1 set the test: if facilitators cannot run a cohort unaided by cycle 2, rollout pauses.

| Role | What they do | Where they come from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lead facilitator | Runs the weekly sessions, makes the go, redesign or stop call, signs off safety | Certified through the route in Gate 2 |
| Second delivery lead | Covers the lead, co-runs sessions, owns data and records | Certified or in training |
| Peer mentor | Supports teams between sessions | Alumni of an earlier cohort |
| Field expert | Reviews Field Cards and challenges projects in their field | Partner university, professional body or employer |
| Partner liaison | Keeps community partners booked and informed | The hub host or the regional partner |
| Hub host | Provides the space and the responsible adult | School, university, library, NGO or community body |

The central team supports facilitators with training, a weekly office hour, review of sample build logs and a channel for urgent safeguarding questions. It does not run sessions in other people's hubs.

How many teams one facilitator can support is the most important delivery number, and it is not known yet. I have not proposed a ratio, because any number would be a guess. The pilot records facilitator time per team each week and compares it with completion and quality of work. The ratio the pilot supports becomes the planning figure, with a safety margin, and it is set again for each new context, because connectivity and learners' starting skills change it.

## Access and equity

The evidence points to the same barriers across regions: devices cost too much, data and travel add up, and some groups are reached far less than others.

- **Devices are the first barrier.** In sub-Saharan Africa the poorest 20% spend an average of [76% of monthly income on a basic smartphone](https://politicaleconomistng.com/beyond-coverage-device-costs-emerge-as-main-barrier-to-nigerias-internet-growth-ai-adoption/), per GSMA data reported in September 2026.
- **Reach is uneven.** The Farmer.Chat deployment found [women, older and rural users faced access barriers](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.08916v2), and it reached 8,805 users in Kenya against 418 in Nigeria.
- **Online-only formats lose people.** [MIT researchers report](https://tsl.mit.edu/research/the-mooc-pivot/) that low MOOC completion has not improved in six years.

The delivery model answers these with five design choices:

1. **Shared devices at hubs,** so that no learner needs to own a device to complete the programme.
2. **Offline-first methods,** so that a lost connection pauses a tool, not the cohort.
3. **Schedule and place options,** so that working students, carers and learners far from the hub can take part, with a hybrid or virtual route where one exists.
4. **Safe access for women and girls,** through hub location and timing, female facilitators or mentors where the community expects it, and travel arrangements agreed with the hub host.
5. **Access reported, not assumed.** Reach and completion are reported by gender, region, age band and connectivity tier, as set in the Gate 1 metrics.

Some costs, such as mobile data for a learner's own device, cannot be removed. Where the programme cannot cover them, it says so to learners before they enrol and does not hide them in a free offer.

## Cost drivers

No prices or costs are set in this document. The table names what each mode will cost money or time for, so the pilot can measure each line. Which modes weigh most on a line is an expectation to test, not a finding.

| Cost driver | What it covers | Weighs most on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Facilitator time | Sessions, preparation, support between sessions, safeguarding duties | All modes; the largest line expected in the pilot |
| Facilitator training and certification | Training, observed practice and the credential | All modes |
| Space | Room hire or in-kind hosting, power, cleaning | Physical and hybrid |
| Devices | Shared devices, charging, replacement | Physical and hybrid |
| Connectivity | Data, a hub connection, virtual session platform | Hybrid and virtual |
| Tools and licences | Paid tools where no free tier fits the age and security rules | Hybrid and virtual |
| Partner and expert time | Community partners, field experts, travel | All modes, often donated |
| Evaluation | The independent evaluator and data collection | All modes |
| Insurance, legal and data protection | Counsel review, insurance, the data protocol | All modes |
| Central team | Curriculum upkeep, training, support, reporting | All modes |

The two figures funders will ask for are worked out the same way in every mode:

```latex
\text{cost per completed learner} = \frac{\text{total cost of the cohort}}{\text{learners who ship a project}}
```

```latex
\text{cost per shipped project} = \frac{\text{total cost of the cohort}}{\text{projects shipped}}
```

The total counts every line above, including time that partners and hosts donate. The pilot reports each figure twice, with and without donated time, so that funders see the real cost of running the programme where nothing is donated.

Who pays follows the Gate 1 model: foundations and development funders for cohorts, schools and universities for the studio licence and facilitator training, and community and employer challenge sponsors for projects. The mix is tested in the pilot.

## Which mode in which pilot context

The Gate 1 brief calls for three contrasting pilot contexts. Each tests a different mode, so that between them the pilot answers the delivery questions.

| Pilot context | Mode and hub tier | What it tests |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mid connectivity (first site) | Hybrid, with a low-bandwidth hub | Whether the method holds when some people are in the room and some are online, and what that costs a facilitator |
| Low connectivity | Physical hub, offline tier, with the virtual layer used only for occasional expert reviews | Whether a cohort can finish the full cycle on paper and offline tools |
| High connectivity | Virtual, live-facilitated, as a second cohort after a hub cohort has proved the method | Whether completion and safeguarding hold with no hub, and whether the learner's own device and data cost excludes people |

The order matters. The mid-connectivity site goes first, as recommended in Gate 4, because it stresses the method least while still testing the hybrid mode. The virtual cohort goes last, because it is the riskiest mode on completion and safeguarding, and the programme should only run it once a facilitator has completed a hub cycle.

If the pilot gives only one site at first, use the mid-connectivity context and add the others as the facilitator team and budget allow.

## Decisions needed and next gate

Four decisions shape Gate 7. My recommendation is in bold.

1. **No self-paced online core:** deliver only through facilitated cohorts in the three modes. **Recommended: yes**, because completion is the largest risk.
2. **Order of the pilot modes:** mid-connectivity hybrid first, then a low-connectivity hub, then a virtual cohort. **Recommended: yes.**
3. **Hub certification:** open a hub only when it has a certified facilitator, a signed data protocol and a named data owner. **Recommended: yes.**
4. **Facilitator ratio:** set no number now, and let the pilot's time records set it. **Recommended: yes.**

Still open from earlier gates: legal form, the pilot site and context, the second delivery lead, the Gate 3, 4 and 5 confirmations, and the domain and trademark checks.
