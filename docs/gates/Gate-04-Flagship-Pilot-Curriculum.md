# Build Beyond Classroom: Flagship Pilot Curriculum (Gate 4)

Oct 14, 2026 · Ekene

## Pilot at a glance

The flagship pilot runs one Community Problem Studio cycle over eight weeks with a facilitated cohort of learners aged 18 and over, on the Build journey. Its job is to test the method, not to prove the programme: it should show which steps hold up, where learners drop out, and what a cycle costs.

| Item | Design |
| --- | --- |
| Learners | Aged 18 and over, university and college students first; graduates and interns welcome |
| Journey and level | Build, Level 2 (Apply and build, rungs 3 to 6), with rungs 1 and 2 taught in the first weeks as fast catch-up |
| Format | Facilitated cohort, in person or hybrid; not self-paced online |
| Rhythm | One facilitated session and one team work block each week; hours set per site |
| Teams | Small teams, proposed at three to five learners, each with its own community partner |
| Output per team | A deployed pilot, a portfolio entry, a one-page business case and a signed handover or stop note |
| Six-week version | Merge weeks 2 and 3, and weeks 5 and 6, for cohorts that cannot give eight weeks |

This curriculum rests on the Gate 3 recommendations, which you have not yet confirmed: the AI use rules, the stop rules, an 18-and-over first cohort and a facilitated format. If you change one, the affected weeks change with it.

Nothing here sets prices, cohort size or numeric targets. Those are set with the pilot site and the independent evaluator before week 1.

## Week by week

Each week names one session focus, the AI skill taught, what each team hands in, and the checkpoint that closes it. Steps refer to the eight Community Problem Studio steps from Gate 3.

| Week | Studio steps | Session focus | New AI skill (ladder rung) | Team hands in | Checkpoint |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Orient, start of 1 | What AI can and cannot do; the build log and verify habits; forming teams | Chat and research with AI (rung 1) | Team charter, first build-log entries, a listening plan | Unaided check: start; readiness build task |
| 2 | 1. Find the need | Listening in the field; telling a need from a solution | Using AI to prepare interviews and group notes (rungs 1 and 2) | Listening Log, draft Need Note, community partner named | Partner confirms the need |
| 3 | 2. Research feasibility | Evidence, existing solutions, limits; verifying AI claims | Research and comparison with source checking (rung 1) | Evidence Pack, Lens Sheet first version | Facilitator's go, redesign or stop call |
| 4 | 3. Ideate, 4. Design | Options, the smallest useful solution, the Solution Spec | Mock-ups and no-code app builders (rungs 2 and 3) | Options Board, Solution Spec, Do-No-Harm Check started | Unaided check: middle; partner reviews the spec |
| 5 | 5. Build (first half) | Building the one user flow | App builders, automation and custom assistants (rungs 3 and 4) | Working core flow, build log up to date | Each member explains a random part with the tool closed |
| 6 | 5. Build (second half), start of 6 | Testing, fixing and safety | AI-assisted coding where the team has the skill, and test generation (rungs 4 and 5) | Test Report, Do-No-Harm Check completed | Facilitator's safety sign-off |
| 7 | 6. Deploy, start of 7 | Putting it in real hands; measuring use | Publishing and simple measurement (rungs 6 and 7) | Live pilot with real users, first user feedback, Pitch Outline | Real users complete the main task |
| 8 | 7. Pitch, 8. Sustain | Business case, pitch, handover | Costing and maintenance planning with AI as tutor (rung 7) | Pitch, Business Case, Sustain Plan, Handover Agreement or stop note | Unaided check: end; owner accepts in writing |

The portfolio each learner owns after week 8 holds the Need Note, Evidence Pack, Solution Spec, build log, Test Report, Business Case and the checkpoint results.

## Session shape and facilitator notes

Every weekly session follows the same five parts, so facilitators learn one pattern and learners know what to expect. Session length is set per site.

1. **Check-in and log review.** Teams report progress against last week's deliverable, and the facilitator spot-reads build logs.
2. **Mini-lesson.** One skill is shown live with the tool on screen, including a failure and how to recover from it.
3. **Studio work.** Teams work on their project while the facilitator circulates and asks questions rather than giving answers.
4. **Partner or peer review.** A community partner or another team challenges the work in progress.
5. **Close.** Each team updates its build log and states its next deliverable.

The table lists the signals a facilitator should act on, and the first move for each.

| Signal | First move |
| --- | --- |
| A team has stayed on one step for more than a week | Ask what evidence it is missing; cut the scope instead of extending the time |
| Output the team cannot explain | Run an unaided mini-check on that part and rebuild it together |
| One member does all the building | Reassign roles and ask the others to demonstrate their parts |
| The community partner does not respond | Help the team find a second partner, and flag it as a pilot risk |
| Connectivity or power fails | Switch to the paper templates and offline tools; log the failure |
| A learner goes quiet or stops attending | Contact them within the week, and record the reason for the completion data |
| AI gives a confident wrong answer | Use it as a live teaching case, and trace the claim to a source together |

Facilitators keep a short weekly note of what held up and what broke. Those notes are the main data the pilot produces about the method itself.

## Explore add-on for pre-university learners

The Explore journey follows the same eight steps but is a different course, so it runs as a smaller, facilitator-led add-on only after local counsel has cleared the age and consent rules in the pilot country. Learners under 13 take part through facilitator-led sessions only, as set in the Gate 2 age bands.

| Element | Build pilot (18 and over) | Explore add-on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Level and rungs | Level 2, rungs 3 to 6 | Level 1, rungs 1 to 3 |
| Problem | Found through the learner's own course or campus | Close to home: a school, family business or neighbourhood |
| Tools | Learners' own accounts where terms allow | Facilitator-run or facilitator-held accounts, with learners directing the work |
| Supervision | Facilitator | Facilitator present throughout, with parent or educator consent on file |
| Business side | Real pilot and a one-page business case | Mock venture, or a real one with guardian awareness, and a simplified lens sheet |
| Field lens | One field, in depth | Several fields sampled, so the learner can compare them |
| Output | Deployed pilot, portfolio, handover | First prototype, a short portfolio entry and a plain statement of how a chosen field links to real work |
| Data | Minimal | Minimal, with no personal data entered into any tool |

The add-on's main purpose is career sensitisation: by the end, a learner can describe what a field looks like as a business and which course or path might suit them. Gate 5 builds the career path engine that this output feeds.

## Assessment and pilot success measures

The pilot assesses two things separately: whether each learner met the standard, and whether the programme worked. Mixing them hides both.

### Learner rubric

A learner earns Level 2 when their team's project and their own unaided work meet every criterion. Safety cannot be traded off against the others: a fail there means the project is redesigned before credit is given.

| Criterion | Meets the standard when |
| --- | --- |
| Need and evidence | The need is confirmed by the partner, and claims in the Evidence Pack trace to sources the learner opened |
| Solution and build | The main user flow works for real users, and the build log shows the learner's own decisions |
| Safety (must pass) | The Do-No-Harm Check is complete, signed off by the facilitator, and no unneeded personal data is collected |
| Business case | The five lens questions are answered for the learner's own field, with sources and honest limits |
| Unaided understanding | The learner explains and extends the work with the tool closed at the middle and end checks |

### Programme measures

These come from the Gate 1 impact table. Targets are set with the evaluator before week 1, not after the results are in.

| Measure | How it is collected | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Completion | Share of enrolled learners who ship a project, with reasons for any who leave | Week 8 |
| Shipped and used | Projects live and used by real people | Week 8, then 90 days after |
| Community benefit | People served, confirmed by the partner | Week 8 and 90 days |
| Business quality | Practices adopted and experiments run, from the Business Case and partner report | Week 8 |
| Unaided learning | Change in unaided check results from start to end | Weeks 1, 4 and 8 |
| Equity | Reach and completion by gender, region and connectivity tier | Week 8 |
| Cost | Cost per learner who completes and per shipped project | After week 8 |
| Facilitator readiness | Whether the facilitator can run the next cycle unaided | Cycle 2 |

The evaluation design, including any comparison group, is agreed with an independent partner before the pilot starts, as the Gate 1 brief requires.

## Signals that mean stop or redesign

These follow the kill-criteria in the Gate 1 brief. The numeric thresholds are set before week 1; the signals and responses are set now.

| Signal | Response |
| --- | --- |
| Completion is below the pre-set target after one redesign | Stop scaling, and rebuild the format around what the dropout reasons show |
| The facilitator cannot run a cohort unaided by cycle 2 | Pause rollout, and extend facilitator training before any new site |
| Counsel flags a legal gap in the pilot country | Pause the affected activity until it is closed |
| Unaided check results do not improve from start to end | Redesign how AI is used in the sessions, starting with steps 2, 7 and 8 |
| Most teams finish without a named owner | Add partner agreement as a condition of starting step 5 |
| Few projects are still used at 90 days | Review the choice of problems and the handover step before a second cohort |
| No partners can be recruited for several teams | Narrow the pilot to the teams that have partners, and record why the others failed |

A stop is a result, not a failure of the pilot. The pilot is designed to produce the evidence a funder needs either way.

## Decisions needed and next gate

Four decisions shape Gate 5. My recommendation is in bold.

1. **Confirm the Gate 3 recommendations** that this curriculum rests on: the AI use rules, the stop rules, an 18-and-over first cohort and a facilitated format. **Recommended: yes.**
2. **Eight weeks or six:** run eight weeks for the first cohort and keep the six-week merge as a fallback. **Recommended: eight**, because the pilot's purpose is to test every step, and compressing hides where it breaks.
3. **Pilot site and context:** name the first site, and whether it is low, mid or high connectivity. **Recommended: start with the mid-connectivity site**, so that the method is tested before it is stressed by the extremes.
4. **Second delivery lead:** name one before the pilot is announced. This is the Gate 1 condition and it is still open.

Still open from earlier gates: legal form and the domain and trademark checks.
