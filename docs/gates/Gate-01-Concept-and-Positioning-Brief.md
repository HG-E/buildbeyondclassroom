# Build Beyond Classroom: Concept & Positioning Brief (Gate 1)

Oct 13, 2026 · Ekene

## Vision

Build Beyond Classroom turns learners in any field into people who can use AI to build, launch and sustain solutions to real community problems.

- **The promise:** every learner leaves with a shipped project, a public portfolio, and a business case for their own field of study.
- **The principle:** value decides what we build, not geography. The programme is designed to work in Lagos, Lima, Mumbai or Manchester, with a universal core and a local layer.
- **The ambition:** a global network of partner schools, universities and community hubs, where solutions proven in one place are documented openly and adapted in another.

## The problem and the gap

Governments are mandating AI literacy and big tech offers free courses, but I found no programme that joins AI building, a real community problem and a business case for each learner's own field. That absence is not proven, only not found in this search.

| Programme | Reach (as reported) | What it covers | What it leaves out |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [School AI mandates](https://techwireasia.com/2026/04/ai-literacy-national-education-workforce/) (UAE, India, China) | UAE KG–12 from 2025–26; India from Grade 3 in 2026–27; China's plan runs to 2030 | AI literacy in class | Applied building, community problems, business |
| [OpenAI Academy](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001270-openai-academy-courses) | 14 free courses, global | Applying AI at work; building for developers | Community problems, field-specific business case; badges are "not certifications" |
| [Google Solution Challenge](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/solution-challenge-2024-using-google-technology-to-address-un-sustainable-development-goals/) | 110+ countries, 4,000+ projects since 2019 (2024 figures) | University students solve UN-goal problems with Google tech | Younger learners, the business case, non-Google tools |
| [Technovation](https://www.raspberrypi.org/blog/ai-education-meaningful-projects-tara-chklovski/) | 350,000+ participants | Teams build AI for community problems | Mainly girls; competition-centred; few impact measures reported |
| [Design for Change](https://en.fundaciontelefonica.com/news/we-take-kiran-sethis-i-can-attitude-to-spain-and-latin-america/) | 35+ countries | Children's social-change projects | AI building and business |
| [Laboratoria](https://www.scotiabank.com/ca/en/about/perspectives.articles.impact.2024-03-laboratoria-latin-america-women-tech-jobs.html) | 11 Latin American countries; about 77.5% of graduates in tech jobs | Bootcamps for women, with job placement | Other fields; paths that are not jobs |

The conditions behind the gap:

- **Adoption lags where it matters.** The IMF's July 2026 study says AI adds only about 0.2% to sub-Saharan Africa's GDP over a decade at current readiness, against about 4% with the right foundations.
- **Skills demand is shifting.** The WEF's 2025 report names AI and big data the fastest-growing skills and analytical thinking the top core skill.
- **Devices, not coverage, block many learners.** In sub-Saharan Africa the poorest 20% spend an average of 76% of monthly income on a basic smartphone, per GSMA data reported in September 2026.

## Who we serve

Anyone with the right mindset, time and resources can join, at any age and in any field. What changes by age is how they access tools and who supervises, not whether they can learn.

| Segment | Starting point | Leaves with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pre-university learners | About to enter campus; unsure of course or career | A first AI-built project, a clear view of how their chosen field connects to real problems and work |
| University and college students | In a course; want practical proof beyond grades | A deployed solution in their own field, a portfolio, an internship-ready business case |
| Graduates, interns and early founders | Seeking a first role or a venture | A scaled or revenue-ready solution, a verified portfolio, a path to funding or employment |
| Educators and community organisations | Run classrooms, clubs or hubs | The method, a facilitator credential, and a studio they can run locally |

Learners under 13 take part through facilitator-led sessions only (see the global architecture section).

## The core: four musts and a capability ladder

Every learner, in any field, must be able to do these four things, and prove it with a shipped project.

1. **Build with AI, not just chat with it.** Produce a working solution: an app, automation, assistant, analysis or content system.
2. **Find and validate a real community problem.** Research it, test feasibility, and think about scale from the start.
3. **Make the business case in their own field.** Their classroom is the case study: who benefits, who pays, what it costs, how it survives.
4. **Own the result.** Verify what AI produced, handle data responsibly, and explain the work without the tool.

The curriculum is a capability ladder, not a tool list. Brands change; the rungs stay.

1. Chat and research with AI
2. Create content and designs
3. Build with no-code and AI app builders
4. Automate workflows and build assistants
5. Code with AI help
6. Deploy and publish (CMS, web, messaging channels)
7. Operate, measure and maintain

Three levels, aligned to [UNESCO's student framework](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/ai-competency-framework-students) (Understand, Apply, Create) and the [OECD and European Commission AI literacy framework](https://digital-skills-jobs.europa.eu/en/latest/news/new-ai-literacy-framework-common-reference-integrating-ai-literacy-education), so schools and funders recognise the structure.

| Level | AI capability | Business depth | Output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Understand and use | Rungs 1–3 | Value and customer | A working prototype for a local need |
| 2. Apply and build | Rungs 3–6 | Model and pilot | A deployed solution used by real people |
| 3. Create and scale | Rungs 5–7 | Scale and sustainability | A solution adapted in a second community, with a funding or revenue path |

Tool rule: tools rotate, so every cohort re-verifies the toolset for cost, age access, security and offline options. Current categories include AI app builders, coding assistants, no-code automation, CMS platforms such as WordPress, and small offline models.

## Positioning

Build Beyond Classroom is the applied layer after AI literacy: where learners of any age and field build, deploy and sustain solutions to real community problems, with the business case for their own discipline.

We partner with the existing players instead of competing with them.

| Existing player | What they do well | Our stance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Schools and ministries | Teach AI literacy at national scale | Complement: they teach understanding, we run applied studios and train facilitators |
| Free big-tech courses | Free, global tool skills | Use as entry and prerequisites; we add the community problem and business case |
| Competitions and challenges | Visibility and prizes for a few teams | Feeder and showcase; every learner ships, not only finalists |
| Bootcamps | Job placement in tech | Pathway for learners who choose a tech career; we serve every field |

What only we combine: any age and field, AI building, a real community problem, a field-specific business case, and open playbooks built to transfer across countries.

## Business model and fundability

The proposed model keeps learner access free or low-cost and has institutions and funders pay for cohorts, with the mix to be validated in the pilot. No prices or revenue figures are set yet.

| Stream | Who pays | What they get | Why it is plausible |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Philanthropic and development funding | Foundations, corporate foundations, development agencies | Measurable youth skills and community outcomes | [Google.org committed $4.6 million](https://www.raspberrypi.org/blog/bringing-ai-education-to-1-25-million-students-across-latin-america/) to scale a free AI literacy programme across Latin America |
| Studio licence for schools and universities | Schools, universities, ministries | Curriculum, facilitator training, project toolkit | School AI mandates create demand; [Design for Change](https://en.fundaciontelefonica.com/news/we-take-kiran-sethis-i-can-attitude-to-spain-and-latin-america/) scaled through teacher training and a foundation partner |
| Community and employer challenges | NGOs, local governments, companies | Learners work on their real problems; a talent pipeline | [Laboratoria](https://www.scotiabank.com/ca/en/about/perspectives.articles.impact.2024-03-laboratoria-latin-america-women-tech-jobs.html) works through employer and donor partners |
| Facilitator certification | Educators, community leaders | A credential to run studios locally | Train-the-trainer is how Experience AI plans to reach 1.25 million students through 24,000 educators |
| Optional learner-side services | Learners with means | Mentorship, verified portfolio | Untested; not part of the core |

Unit economics to measure in the pilot (targets set before it starts):

- Cost per learner who completes
- Cost per shipped project
- Learners per facilitator
- Share of projects still used by real people after 90 days

What funders will ask for: a theory of change, an independent evaluation, open playbooks, a child-data policy, and local ownership of delivery.

## Impact and success metrics

Success is measured by shipped, used and sustained solutions and by what learners can do unaided, not by enrolment alone.

| Metric | Why it matters | How it is measured |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Completion | Online-only courses lose most learners; [MIT researchers report low MOOC completion has not improved in 6 years](https://tsl.mit.edu/research/the-mooc-pivot/) | Share of enrolled learners who ship a project |
| Shipped and used solutions | Proof of building, not attendance | Projects live and used by real people at 90 days |
| Community benefit | The reason the programme exists | People served, confirmed by the community partner, not only by learners |
| Business quality | [A Uganda trial found trained youth's firms had about 20% higher revenue and 16% higher profit after about nine years](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34637), driven by better practices | Practices adopted, experiments run, revenue or cost savings where relevant |
| Unaided learning | [A July 2026 experiment found gains held when AI tutored and faded when AI wrote for students](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08849) | Short unaided checks at the start, middle and end |
| Equity | Women, older and rural users faced access barriers in [Farmer.Chat's deployment](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.08916v2) | Reach and completion by gender, region and connectivity tier |
| Cost | Fundability depends on it | Cost per completed learner and per shipped project |

Honest limits: the service-learning evidence base ([62 studies, effects of 0.27 to 0.43](https://www.tamiu.edu/profcenter/documents/Meta-AnalysisoftheImpactofSLonStudentts_2011.pdf)) relies mostly on self-report, and the AI-and-learning evidence is early. The pilot should agree its evaluation design with an independent partner before it starts.

## Risks and kill-criteria

The biggest risks are low completion, a rollout that outruns facilitator capacity, and under-age data and tool-access rules. Each has a signal that tells us to change course. Numeric targets are set before the pilot, not after.

| Risk | Evidence | Mitigation | Change-course signal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Learners drop out | Low MOOC completion has not improved in 6 years | Facilitated cohorts, hubs, peer teams | Completion below the pre-set target after one redesign |
| Rollout outruns readiness | [South Korea's US$850 million AI textbook initiative collapsed within four months](https://techwireasia.com/2026/04/ai-literacy-national-education-workforce/) | Start small; train facilitators first | Facilitators cannot run a cohort unaided by cycle 2 |
| Under-age access and data law | [India's DPDP Act treats under-18s as children](https://ksandk.com/md/data-protection-and-data-privacy/child-data-protection-under-dpdp-act-parental-consent-rules/); [GDPR sets consent between 13 and 16](https://bastion.tech/learn/gdpr/childrens-data-protection) | Facilitator-led use for minors, minimal data, no tracking, parent or educator consent | Counsel flags a gap in any target country |
| AI replaces learning | Gains faded when AI wrote for students; UAE educators warn of [cognitive offloading](https://courier.unesco.org/en/articles/ai-goes-school-united-arab-emirates) | AI as tutor and co-builder; unaided checkpoints | Unaided scores do not improve |
| Solutions do not last | [Farmer.Chat had 418 users in Nigeria against 8,805 in Kenya](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.08916v2) | Every project needs a named local owner and a sustain plan | Few projects still used at 90 days |
| Tool lock-in and cost | Prices and age rules change often | Tool-agnostic ladder; offline options; re-verify each cohort | Core course depends on one vendor |
| Key-person risk | Common in founder-led programmes | Core team and advisory board before the pilot | Pilot cannot start without a second delivery lead |

## Name, tagline and domain

Keep "Build Beyond Classroom" as the working name: it is active, says the programme goes past school, and fits every field. Its weakness is that "classroom" may translate poorly or suggest schools only, so test it with partners in several languages.

Tagline options:

- Learn it. Build it. Scale it.
- Your field. Your community. Your build.
- Build what your community needs.

Not yet checked: availability of BuildBeyondClassroom.com and .org, trademark conflicts in target markets, and social handles. All three must be verified before any public launch.

## Decisions needed and next gate

Five decisions shape Gate 2 (programme architecture):

1. Confirm the positioning statement and the four musts.
2. Choose the legal form: non-profit, social enterprise or a hybrid. Fundability and revenue options depend on it.
3. Choose three contrasting pilot contexts, for example one low-connectivity, one mid and one high, across different regions.
4. Name a second delivery lead and a first advisory board.
5. Verify the name, domain and trademarks.

Next gate: Gate 2, the programme architecture (tracks, levels and learner journeys for pre-university, student and intern), after you approve this brief and its three drawings.
