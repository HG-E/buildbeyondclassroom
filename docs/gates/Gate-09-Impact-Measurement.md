# Impact Measurement (Gate 9)

Oct 15, 2026 · Ekene

## What this gate decides

BBC counts impact as shipped, used solutions that a community partner confirms, not attendance or satisfaction. This gate turns the seven measures in the Gate 1 brief into indicators with a named evidence source, a capture point inside the studio steps, and a reporting route.

- **Three levels:** the learner, the community, and the programme (hubs and facilitators).
- **Capture:** built into the eight studio steps and the facilitator's existing sign-offs, on paper first, then on the Gate 7 platform.
- **Evaluation:** designed with an independent evaluator before week 1, with honest limits on what the pilot can claim.
- **Ethics:** no ranking of learners, no profiling, minimal data, small groups never reported alone.
- **Pilot rule:** numeric targets are set with the evaluator before week 1, not after the results are in.

## The results chain

BBC measures four links from studio activity to lasting effect, and counts impact at the third, where a community partner can confirm it.

The first two links are the learner's own evidence. The third needs the community partner's confirmation at step 8 and again at day 90. The fourth is followed up after the pilot and is not claimed from it.

## How data is captured without extra burden

Nothing is measured that a facilitator is not already doing; each indicator rides on an existing step, form or sign-off.

1. **Paper first.** The Cycle Plan, build log, unaided-check sheet, partner confirmation and incident log are printed forms in the facilitator kit.
2. **One hub sheet at v0.** The facilitator or hub lead copies the paper results into a single spreadsheet per cohort, as Gate 4 and Gate 7 planned. No learner accounts and no analytics on the site.
3. **Records in the database from v1.** Sign-offs, consent and portfolio entries are stored as set out in Gate 7, with the same minimal fields.
4. **Partner confirmations by short form.** The community partner signs a one-page confirmation at step 8 and again at day 90. A phone call recorded by the facilitator counts if the partner cannot write.
5. **No extra survey for learners during the cohort.** The unaided checks and the portfolio give the learner evidence; a short exit conversation is led by the facilitator.

A 90-day follow-up is the one added task. The facilitator or hub lead contacts the community partner, because the Gate 1 measure of a live, used solution cannot be seen earlier.

## Evaluation design and honest limits

The pilot can show whether the method works in three contrasting settings; it cannot show lasting income or employment effects, and BBC will say so.

**Design**

- An independent evaluator agrees the design, the indicators and the numeric targets before week 1, as Gates 1 and 4 require.
- The first unaided check and the enrolment counts are the baseline; the middle and end checks show change.
- A comparison group is used only if the evaluator finds a fair one, such as the next cohort on a waiting list. Small pilots may not support one, and that is stated in the report.
- Several kinds of evidence are combined: counts, partner confirmations, facilitator observation and review of portfolios.

**What BBC will not claim**

- That eight weeks changes a learner's income or employment. The Gate 1 evidence on business results comes from a trial that followed trained youth for about nine years.
- That the findings hold in every country after one pilot.
- That AI use improved learning. The claim is limited to whether unaided check results rose.
- Community benefit based only on what learners report.

The service-learning evidence cited in Gate 1 relies mostly on self-report, and the evidence on AI and learning is early. The evaluation should therefore weight partner confirmations and unaided checks above learner opinion.

## Measuring ethically with young people

Measurement follows the same child-data rules as the rest of BBC, and no indicator is worth breaking them.

1. **Separate consent.** Consent to measurement is separate from consent to take part. A learner can join the cohort and decline use of their data in research.
2. **No ranking.** Unaided checks are for learning, not for grades, selection or league tables of learners, teams or hubs.
3. **Small-number rule.** A group below a minimum size is never reported alone; it is folded into a larger group. The minimum is set with the evaluator and counsel.
4. **Pseudonyms.** The evaluator receives IDs, not names, and no date of birth, school or address.
5. **Age.** The first pilot is for learners aged 18 and over, as Gate 4 set. No measurement of under-18s starts until counsel has cleared the Explore add-on.
6. **No named data to partners or funders.** They receive counts and confirmed outcomes only.
7. **Learner first.** A learner sees their own results before anyone else, can export them, and can correct or withdraw them.
8. **No profiling.** Results are never used to infer traits about a learner or predict their future.

## Reporting

Each audience sees only what it needs, and named learner data never leaves the learner, their facilitator and core admin.

| Audience | What they see | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Learner | Their own unaided checks, portfolio and rung reached | After each check; exportable at any time |
| Facilitator | Their cohort's progress and signals from the Gate 4 stop-or-redesign table | Weekly |
| Hub host and regional partner | Cohort counts for completion, equity and safeguarding; no names | End of each cohort |
| Community partner | The solution's status and the confirmation they signed | Step 8 and day 90 |
| Funders | Aggregate results against pre-set targets, cost per completed learner and per shipped project, the independent evaluator's findings | Mid-pilot and end of pilot |
| Public | An open pilot report with results, cost and what did not work, plus the updated playbook | After each pilot |

Funders already told us what they will ask for in Gate 1: a theory of change, an independent evaluation, open playbooks, a child-data policy and local ownership of delivery. This reporting set answers each, and the evaluator's findings are published whether or not they flatter the programme, as clause 7 of the Gate 8 agreement protects.

## Pilot measurement plan and decision rules

Every stop-or-redesign signal in Gate 4 is detected by one indicator, checked at a set time, against a threshold fixed before week 1.

| Gate 4 signal | Indicator that detects it | Checked | Threshold |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Completion below target after one redesign | Completion | End of each cycle | Set with the evaluator before week 1 |
| Facilitator cannot run a cohort unaided by cycle 2 | Facilitator readiness | Cycle 2 | Set before week 1 |
| Counsel flags a legal gap | Counsel review log | Continuous | Any open gap pauses that activity |
| Unaided check results do not improve | Unaided learning | Start, middle and end | Set before week 1 |
| Most teams finish without a named owner | Named owner | Steps 5 and 8 | Set before week 1 |
| Few projects still used at 90 days | Solution live and used | Day 90 | Set before week 1 |
| No partners recruited for several teams | Partner recorded per team | Step 1 | Set before week 1 |

**Before week 1**

- [ ] Independent evaluator appointed and the design agreed in writing.
- [ ] Numeric thresholds set for every row above and recorded.
- [ ] Paper forms printed: Cycle Plan, build log, unaided-check sheet, partner confirmation, incident log.
- [ ] Measurement consent form ready, separate from the participation consent.
- [ ] One hub spreadsheet set up with pseudonymous IDs.
- [ ] Day-90 follow-up dates booked with each community partner.

## Decisions to confirm and open items

Five decisions need a yes before the pilot's measurement is fixed, and five items are still open.

**Decisions (recommended: yes to all)**

- [ ] Impact is counted as shipped, used solutions confirmed by the community partner, with the eleven indicators above.
- [ ] Capture is paper first and one hub spreadsheet at v0, with no learner accounts and no analytics on the site.
- [ ] An independent evaluator agrees the design and the numeric thresholds before week 1.
- [ ] Measurement consent is separate from participation consent, with no ranking or profiling.
- [ ] The 90-day follow-up with each community partner is part of the pilot, and the evaluator's findings are published in full.

**Open items**

- The independent evaluator is not yet named.
- Numeric thresholds are not set; the evaluator and you set them together.
- The minimum group size for reporting is not set; counsel and the evaluator confirm it.
- Counsel has not confirmed which data protection rules govern measurement in the pilot country.
- From earlier gates: legal form, the first pilot site, a second delivery lead, and the name, trademark and domain checks.
